# Sample FAQ — Living in Germany (illustrative demo content)

> legal advice. Replace it with your own real documents (insurance letters,
> Arztbriefe, rental contracts, official PDFs) in `data/documents/`.

## Address registration (Anmeldung)
When you move to a new address in Germany you must register it (Anmeldung) at the
local citizens' office (Bürgeramt / Einwohnermeldeamt). In most cities the
deadline is **14 days** after moving in. You need your passport, the rental
confirmation from your landlord (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung), and a completed
registration form.

## Health insurance (Krankenversicherung)
Health insurance is mandatory in Germany. Statutory insurers (gesetzliche
Krankenkassen) such as TK, AOK, and Barmer cover most residents. Students under
30 usually qualify for reduced student rates. Keep your insurance card
(Gesundheitskarte) with you for doctor visits.

## Doctor's letter (Arztbrief)
An Arztbrief is a letter your doctor writes summarising a diagnosis, findings,
and recommended treatment, often addressed to another doctor. It typically
contains sections for Diagnose (diagnosis), Befund (findings), and Therapie
(treatment). Bring it to any follow-up appointment.

## Sick note (Krankmeldung / AU)
If you are ill, a doctor can issue a certificate of incapacity to work
(Arbeitsunfähigkeitsbescheinigung, "AU"). You must inform your employer on the
first day of sickness. For absences of more than **3 days**, most employers
require the AU certificate.

## Residence permit renewal
A residence permit (Aufenthaltstitel) must be renewed before it expires. Book an
appointment at the immigration office (Ausländerbehörde) several weeks in advance,
as slots fill up. Bring your passport, proof of income or enrolment, and proof of
health insurance.
